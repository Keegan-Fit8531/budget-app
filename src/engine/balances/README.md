# engine/balances/

What payday sends each account, and what's in each account over time. An account's **payday mode**
(`SplitLine.mode` in `core/types.ts`) decides how its amount is worked out:
- **fixed**: a set amount every pay.
- **exact**: that pay's own costs, debt payments, money put aside and goal amounts.
- **smooth** ("Steady"): a level amount, worked out over a long run of pays, so 4- and 5-week pays even out.
- **build** ("Build up"): each payday shares what's due up to the next 5-week pay evenly over the paydays before it, less what's in the account; back to about $0 after each 5-week pay.

## Files
- `deposits.ts`: **the one place to ask what payday sends**: `depositFor(budget, accountId, period)`. Order: for a locked pay, a one-pay adjustment (`overrides`) that differs from the frozen split, else the frozen split; then a one-pay adjustment; then the account's mode (`liveDepositFor`). Also `depositSansBalance` and `depositPending` (a balance dated on payday that doesn't include the deposit yet).
- `outflows.ts`: money leaving an account between two dates (also `keepsBuffer`: Steady or Build up): `outflows` (costs and debt payments; money put aside stays, so it isn't here), `fundedOut` (what payday has to fund, including put-aside amounts), `exactNeed` (what exact mode sends), `modeOf` / `accountMode`. Gem Visa money held in another account until statement day is handled here: at least Gem's recommended amount (never more than it's projected to still owe), unless you set the statement's amount yourself (`Debt.heldPay`, from the bill check), which replaces it.
- `steady.ts`: steady amounts. A "run" goes pay by pay from the balance you entered (about 40 pays), each pay's amount worked out from what's projected to be left before it. `steadyFor`, `smoothAmount` (what Deposits shows), `levelAmount` (for Balance this pay: the amount that keeps every pay in the next 12 at the usual amount). Amounts are to the cent, or rounded up to the account's rounding (`Account.round`, e.g. Rent in whole dollars: `steadyStep`, `usualSteady`). Runs are cached per budget object and per input (`cached`, `runKey`).
- `freeze.ts`: once a pay is locked (payday has passed, or its pay or payslip is in), what payday sent is frozen in `budget.splitSent` so automatic changes (balancing, a debt paid off) don't rewrite it. The freeze takes in one-pay adjustments there at the time, so code that removes one by hand has to update `splitSent` too, or the frozen figure keeps the amount (`features/planner/logic/thisPay.ts` and `allocate.ts` → `undoAllocation` do, with `keepSplitChange`: see `state/README.md`). `paySplitLocked`, `freezePaySplit`, `freezePaySplitFrom`, `freezeLockedPays`, `unfreezePaySplit`.
- `estimate.ts`: balances over time from the last balance you entered: `estimateBalance`, `balanceToday`, `balanceOn`, `forecastBalances` (Next 6 pays), `unpaidByBalanceDay` / `outByBalance` (below), `envelopeSpentBy` (envelope money counted as spent by a date: take it off a future balance, as the Planner's Ends does).
- `projection.ts`: what an account can spare (`spareBalance`, `spareInfo`) and a pay day by day (`payProjection`).
- `build.ts`: Build up amounts: `buildAt` (one payday: the stretch ends at the first pay needing more than `averageNeed`; earlier pays an even share rounded to `buildStep`, the account's rounding or $10; that pay exactly what's left), `buildFor` (pay by pay from the entered balance, reusing Steady's run: `runStart`, `window`, `fromRun`). `build.test.ts` tests it.
- `buffer.ts`: a Steady or Build up account's **Buffer** (Rent: the Accounts card and the Planner pill): `potBuffer` runs the next 12 pays (`BUFFER_PAYS`, a year) from what's left after a pay's bills, each pay's deposit less its bills, and holds the lowest the running total falls below zero (`runningLow`); `free` = left − buffer, negative = short for that pay. `bufferLine` says it in words. A later pay starts from its own projected end, so the surplus before it counts.
- `billTracker.ts`: the live bill tracker for the current pay (Accounts): each bill paid or still to come against what's in the account (`billTracker`); for a bank-connected account the bank decides what's paid (`engine/billMatch.ts`, `late` once due). A held debt's payment (Gem) is a row with `debtId`, in `toCome` until the bank shows it; part-paid, the rest is its own row (`rest`, `splitPartPaid`). Also `toMove` (bills paid on another account's card whose money is still here) and `moves`.
- `topUps.ts`: exact accounts that pay bank bills top up (one holding Gem, like Misc bills, once its balance is entered): whatever's left from earlier pays counts towards the next (`topsUp`, `exactTopUp`), and what was in an account before the budget's first pay (`leftoverBeforeBudget`).
- `balances.test.ts`: frozen splits, payday morning before the deposit lands, the bill tracker. `buffer.test.ts`: the running-balance buffer. `outflows.test.ts`: Misc bills sends only each pay's need (leftover used, Gem paid off, a set-aside at its max).

## Watch out for
- A debt held in another account (Gem in Misc bills) is handled across several files here: `features/debts/logic/held/README.md` says how.
- These functions call each other a lot (deposits ↔ outflows ↔ steady ↔ projection). When you add one, put it in the file whose job it matches and import it directly from there.
- The run cache relies on the store cloning the budget for each change. Don't mutate a budget in place and then expect fresh figures from the same object in the same render.
- A steady account's buffer is a running balance, not each pay's own gap: 4-payment pays' spare covers a 5-payment pay after them, but several 5-payment pays close together can need more than one pay's gap (`buffer.test.ts`).
- A run covers 40 pays; a pay further out gets a longer run to the end of its year (`runLength`'s `until`), never the 40th's amount repeated.
- Runs (`steady.ts` → `runStart`: Steady, Build up, exact top-ups) start from the balance less envelope money still unspent, money put aside up to its max (Haircut $60), and bills up to the balance's day the bank hasn't shown yet (`estimate.ts` → `unpaidByBalanceDay`, as the bill tracker lists them): counted as a cushion, the next pay sends too little.
- The bank's balance often arrives a day before its lines (rent out, balance $415 lower, no line yet). `outByBalance` counts such a bill paid when the balance fell by it since the previous synced balance (`accountHistory`) and the lines since don't explain the drop; the tracker and `unpaidByBalanceDay` both use it, or the card says short by the bill and the next pay sends it again.
- `today` is a parameter almost everywhere (default `todayISO()`), so tests can fix the date. Keep passing it through.
